# FINAL LOFT ADVISORY BRAND IDENTITY SYSTEM

## Brand Character

LOFT Advisory should feel refined, composed, intelligent, warm, and quietly confident.
The identity should communicate experience and judgment without falling into conventional healthcare consulting aesthetics. LOFT should feel established and highly considered, but never institutional, corporate, overly feminine, decorative, or trend-driven.
Elegance should come from typography, spacing, proportion, materials, and restraint rather than overt luxury cues.
The overall impression should be that of a sophisticated private advisory firm with an editorial sensibility.

## Logo System

### Primary Logo

The primary logo uses the finalized heavier Bodoni Moda LOFT wordmark with ADVISORY positioned asymmetrically beneath it. Preserve the offset, spacing, and proportions of the supplied artwork; do not retype or reconstruct it. Bodoni Moda is the logo treatment, not the site heading font.
The offset placement is intentional. It gives the mark a more distinctive and editorial composition than a conventional centered lockup.
Primary logo color: Ink Blue #2B4057. The user approved the supplied artwork color as the standard during this cleanup; it replaces the former Deep Logo Navy logo specification.

### Responsive Logo

The LOFT-only wordmark is the approved responsive version of the identity.
Use the LOFT-only mark when the full lockup becomes too small for ADVISORY to remain clearly legible.
Do not continue shrinking the full lockup once the descriptor begins to disappear.

### Approved Logo Versions

Full LOFT Advisory, #2B4057, for light backgrounds.
LOFT-only, #2B4057, for small digital applications.
Full LOFT Advisory, #F7F3EC, for dark backgrounds.
LOFT-only, #F7F3EC, for small applications on dark backgrounds.
SVG should be the preferred digital format when supported. Transparent PNG files may be used where SVG is unavailable.
Retain a vector master for professional print production.

### Logo Treatment

The logo should remain restrained.
Do not recolor it with Aubergine, add decorative rules, stretch or compress the mark, change the relationship between LOFT and ADVISORY, reduce opacity, or add effects such as shadows, gradients, outlines, or glows.
The identity gets its color and expression from the broader visual system rather than from altering the logo itself.

## Color System

LOFT uses a neutral-led palette.
The creams and taupe create the visual environment. Navy provides structure. Aubergine introduces selective depth and distinction.
The brand should not feel like a navy-and-plum identity placed on cream.
It should feel like a cream-and-neutral identity punctuated by navy and plum.

### Core Palette

Soft Ivory #F7F3EC: Primary brand canvas. Use for primary website backgrounds, large open areas, stationery, editorial layouts, light-background logo applications. This should be the dominant visual color throughout the identity.

Champagne Cream #EEE6DA: Secondary neutral. Use for alternating website sections, cards, content groupings, document panels, and subtle visual separation.

Cool Taupe #D4CBC0: Strongest neutral. Use selectively for cards, diagrams, secondary content blocks, dividers, and supporting structure. It should not dominate large portions of a layout.

Charcoal Brown #34302D: Primary body-text color. Use instead of pure black whenever possible. It integrates more naturally with the warm-neutral system while maintaining strong readability.

Ink Blue #2B4057: Primary accent color. Use for major headings, important section titles, primary buttons, graphic structure, selected numbers, and occasional dark sections. This color should remain visibly blue.

Aubergine #65505F: Secondary accent. Use sparingly for eyebrow text, small section markers, Blueprint details, selected numbers, subtle callouts, and minor interactive emphasis. Aubergine should behave more like a finishing detail than a second primary color.

Deep Navy #24384A: Functional primary-button hover color retained from the existing UI guidance. It is no longer the logo or icon color and is not part of the six-color primary palette.

### Color Hierarchy

Soft Ivory and Champagne first → Charcoal structure → Ink Blue emphasis → Aubergine detail.
Most LOFT layouts should be dominated by the neutral family.
Large saturated fields should be occasional rather than routine.

### Dark Backgrounds

Primary dark field: Ink Blue #2B4057. This is the preferred dark background for website sections, presentation slides, and major branded moments. Use Soft Ivory text and logo on top.

Secondary dark field: Aubergine #65505F. Approved for selective feature sections, callouts, quotations, or special moments. It should remain uncommon enough that it retains impact.

Light text on dark fields: Soft Ivory #F7F3EC. Avoid pure white unless a particular application requires it.
Use Ink Blue for the primary dark field; the deeper functional hover color is not a logo color.

## Typography

LOFT uses two Google Fonts for site typography: Lora and DM Sans. The heavier Bodoni Moda logo is supplied artwork and does not change this site font pairing.
Lora carries the expressive and editorial side of the identity. Use it for hero headlines, major section headings, selected subheadings, pull quotes, Blueprint stage titles, selected display numerals, and intentional uppercase labels. Lora should generally remain at Regular weight. It works particularly well in uppercase and can be used where the brand needs a more distinctive typographic moment.

DM Sans handles functional communication. Use it for body copy, navigation, buttons, captions, interface text, forms, tables, financial information, and data-heavy content. Use Regular as the default weight. Use Medium selectively for navigation, buttons, small labels, and functional hierarchy. Avoid SemiBold and Bold unless a real legibility requirement makes them necessary.

### Typographic Principle

The serif carries the personality. The sans-serif carries the information. The overall system should feel light rather than heavy. With the cream-led palette, excessive typographic weight creates unnecessary visual density.

Suggested desktop hierarchy:

- Hero Heading: Lora Regular, 56–64 px, line height 1.05–1.12.
- Section Heading: Lora Regular, 34–42 px.
- Body Copy: DM Sans Regular, 17–19 px, line height 1.5–1.6.
- Eyebrow or Small Label: Lora Regular or DM Sans Medium, 12–14 px.
- Button: DM Sans Medium, 15–16 px.
- Caption / Fine Print: DM Sans Regular, 13–14 px.

Numerals: Lora numerals may be used in display or editorial contexts. DM Sans should be used for functional numbers such as tables, financial information, forms, dates, dashboards, and operational data.

### Capitalization

All caps should be used deliberately.
Lora performs especially well in uppercase and may be used for labels such as INDEPENDENT PRACTICE ADVISORY, THE LOFT BLUEPRINT, and DISCOVER.
DM Sans should generally remain in sentence case or title case. Large amounts of uppercase DM Sans can feel too mechanical for the identity.

## Layout Philosophy

LOFT layouts should feel spacious, controlled, and deliberate.
Use generous margins, restrained line lengths, strong hierarchy, and enough negative space for elements to breathe.
Do not fill space simply because it is available.
Typography and spacing should do much of the organizational work.
Avoid excessive use of cards, borders, icons, decorative fills, or visual containers when simpler composition will communicate the information clearly.
Asymmetry is welcome when it feels intentional.

## Background Usage

The default LOFT environment should not be bright white.
Use Soft Ivory #F7F3EC as the preferred primary canvas.
Champagne Cream and Cool Taupe should create subtle shifts between content areas.
Pure white may still be appropriate inside forms, spreadsheets, operational documents, dashboards, or other functional environments.

## Buttons and UI

LOFT interface elements should feel structural rather than soft.
Avoid pill buttons, large rounded corners, floating-card effects, gradients, and soft shadows.

Primary Button: background Ink Blue #2B4057; text Soft Ivory #F7F3EC; DM Sans Medium; 15–16 px; padding approx. 14 px vertical / 24 px horizontal; radius 2 px. Hover: Deep Navy #24384A.

Secondary Button: transparent background; 1 px Ink Blue border; Ink Blue text; 2 px radius. Hover: Champagne Cream #EEE6DA fill.

Text Links: Ink Blue by default. Underline or otherwise increase visual distinction on hover. Aubergine may be used selectively for minor interactive emphasis but should not become the primary CTA color.

Selected / Active State: Cool Taupe #D4CBC0 background; Ink Blue text.

Form Fields: Soft Ivory background; Cool Taupe 1 px border; Charcoal Brown text; 2 px radius. Focus state should use a restrained Ink Blue outline.

UI principle: Straight edges, thin borders, restrained contrast, minimal decoration.

## Graphic Language

Graphic elements should remain minimal.
Appropriate elements include fine rules, controlled blocks of color, generous whitespace, numbered markers, and subtle typographic emphasis.
Avoid decorative flourishes, swashes, gradients, excessive rounded shapes, and trendy abstract graphics.
The brand should feel designed without constantly calling attention to the design.

## Iconography

Icons should be simple and functional.
Use line icons with consistent stroke weight and restrained geometry.
Icons should support comprehension rather than become a defining visual motif.
Preferred colors: Charcoal Brown, Ink Blue, occasional Aubergine.
Avoid overly friendly, cartoon-like, filled, or decorative icon systems.

## Photography Direction

LOFT photography should feel editorial, intelligent, calm, and real.
Photography should support the identity rather than dominate it.

Preferred characteristics: natural or architectural light, restrained neutral color, thoughtful composition, negative space, authentic professional environments, independent clinical settings, working details and materials, natural human interaction, selective close crops of hands, documents, architecture, or tools.

People: When people appear, they should look engaged in real work rather than posed for a corporate photo. Candid or lightly directed imagery is preferable to formal stock-style portraiture. Expressions should feel natural and focused rather than exaggeratedly cheerful.

Avoid: stereotypical healthcare marketing imagery such as physicians with crossed arms, generic smiling teams, staged handshakes, stethoscope close-ups, bright hospital environments, and obviously staged clinical scenes. Also avoid overly beige lifestyle photography or imagery that attempts to signal luxury for its own sake.

Color treatment: Photography should sit naturally beside #F7F3EC, #EEE6DA, #D4CBC0, #2B4057, #65505F. Warmth is welcome, but skin tones and whites should remain natural. Avoid heavy orange warmth, teal shadows, aggressive desaturation, or obvious preset-style grading.

Website usage: Photography should be used relatively sparingly. LOFT should derive most of its identity from typography, spacing, color, and composition. A strong image in the right place is preferable to an image in every section.

## The LOFT Blueprint

The LOFT Blueprint is a branded framework within the LOFT identity, not a separate sub-brand.
It should use the same typography and palette.
Blueprint stage labels and numbers are good places to introduce Ink Blue or restrained Aubergine.
For example, 01 DISCOVER may appear in Lora when used editorially. DM Sans may be used when the same information appears inside the client portal or another functional interface.
The Blueprint should feel systematic without becoming visually rigid.

## Brand Voice

LOFT should sound like the same firm it looks like.
The voice should be natural, direct, warm, knowledgeable, measured, and professional.
Avoid consultant jargon, inflated claims, buzzwords, overly symmetrical marketing language, and polished phrasing that feels impersonal.
LOFT should sound like an experienced advisor speaking clearly to another accomplished professional.

## Print Direction

The identity is especially well suited to tactile print production.
Preferred materials and techniques include thick uncoated or cotton stock, blind embossing or debossing, letterpress, restrained foil applications, matte finishes, and high-quality paper textures.
The logo should be allowed to benefit from material and production quality rather than additional decoration.

## Digital Logo Behavior

The revised wordmark has additional optical weight so the fine serif details remain visible on screen.
For larger applications, use the full LOFT Advisory lockup.
At smaller sizes, switch to the LOFT-only wordmark.
Do not shrink the full lockup until ADVISORY becomes visual noise.
Legibility takes priority over forcing one logo version into every application.

## Brand Design Test

Before approving any LOFT design, ask: Does this feel calm, intelligent, refined, and intentional?
If something is visually competing for attention without improving understanding, hierarchy, or meaning, it probably does not belong.
That is the central principle of the LOFT Advisory visual identity.
